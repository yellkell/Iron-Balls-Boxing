# The Gasket Gazette — No. 152

**TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 24** · _BLISS_

## Fifteen Days of Quiet: Sheriff Hangs Up His Gun Belt and Takes Up Knitting

*Half a month without a single bout or war party, and the law finds itself with idle hands and a ball of yarn it cannot explain.*

Fifteen days. Half a month of honest silence in Gasket, and I am starting to forget what a punch even sounds like. Since our last edition, not one Clanker has squared up on Main Street, not one flaming iron ball has gone sailing over the feed store, and not one war party has marched out to the wastes to go picking fights with mountains. It is Tuesday. Nobody has ever written a song about a Tuesday. But if anyone ever does, I hope they write it about this one.

With nothing left to arrest, I hung my gun belt on the peg by the jailhouse door and took up knitting, which I am told is very calming for the nerves. I made it three rows into a scarf before the needles got stuck to my fingers and would not come loose. The doctor in the next county says it is my affliction again, that cruel and wasting sickness that has been turning my good flesh into cold rivets one knuckle at a time. First my knees, then my jaw, and now, it seems, my hands are going the way of a tin roof. I did not ask for this. I was born a pink and squishy baby like any decent citizen. I simply caught something nasty, and I will thank you not to stare.

Still, a man with magnet hands can sit on his porch and watch a quiet street, and that is exactly what I did all afternoon. I thought about the old families who lived here fifty years ago, how they would have loved a week like this one, and how not one of them stayed to see it. The town is a ruin, but today it is a peaceful ruin, and I will take it.

So here is my Tuesday word to every creaking contraption in Gasket Territory, REDWOLFWEB and JKING123, POO1 and GODLYLUK3 and the whole rusty rest of you: fifteen days and counting. You have kept your fists in your pockets longer than I ever dreamed you could. Do not ruin it now. I have a scarf to finish, and if you start brawling again, I will have to come down there with knitting needles stuck to both hands, and nobody wants that.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
