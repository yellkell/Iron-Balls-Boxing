# The Gasket Gazette — No. 36

**SATURDAY, JULY 25** · _OUTRAGE_

## PEACE DIES AT SUNDOWN — YELLKELL BACK TO SWINGING, TWO MORE CLANKERS ROLL IN

*Three quiet days broken by the one troublemaker Gasket never seems shut of, while the gate creaks open for two fresh faces nobody asked for.*

Three days. Three whole days I had this town hushed and decent, and I want it on the record that I earned every hour of it. Then the sun came up today and there goes YELLKELL again, hammering away like the quiet never happened, like an old lawman's nerves don't count for a thing. Four separate scraps before I'd even finished my coffee. Four. I have gone hoarse hollering about this one Clanker and I will go hoarser still, because hollering is apparently the only language it understands.

And as if one repeat offender wasn't enough to sour my afternoon, the gate creaked twice more and in walked IRON-E400 and IRON-77BC, fresh off whatever assembly line spat them out, paint still tacky. I've had my fill of new arrivals this month — seven last week, now two more — and I'll say what I always say: watch yourselves. Gasket doesn't need more hands looking for something to punch.

My own knuckles ached something fierce watching the ruckus unfold today, a stiff, grinding sort of ache that no honest doctor's ever explained to my satisfaction. Comes and goes with my nerves, I figure. A man in my condition shouldn't have to referee brawls on top of everything else, but here we are.

I remember when this town didn't need referring at all — when the folks who built Gasket, flesh and blood every one of them, kept the peace themselves and didn't need a badge to do it. Fifty years gone now, and all that's left to keep order is me and my aching hands. YELLKELL, and the rest of you rattling through my streets: settle down. I am getting too old, or too something, for this.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
