# The Gasket Gazette — No. 61

**WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 19** · _GLEE_

## NOT A SPARK, NOT A DENT — GASKET GOES A FULL DAY WITHOUT A SINGLE CLANKER RAISING A FIST

*Your Sheriff searched high and low for trouble and, praise be, could not find a lick of it.*

Friends, I want you to sit with this the way I did: a whole day come and gone in Gasket, and not one flaming ball left a fist in anger. No bouts. No brawls. No posse marching out past the town line to go pick a fight with something twelve times its size, either — the wastes stayed as quiet as Main Street, which is to say, blessedly, perfectly still. I read the wire report twice through, certain some fool had slipped past me, and found nothing but empty columns. I near wept.

I'll admit the quiet does strange things to a man of my condition. Sat on the porch this morning and felt every plate and rivet in this cursed, wasting body of mine ease up a notch, like the good Lord himself reached down and loosened a bolt behind my ribs. The doctors keep telling me it's just the affliction creeping further along, turning good honest flesh to cold metal one joint at a time — a cruel disease, and no fault of my own, whatever the wiseacres in this town like to whisper. But today, affliction and all, I felt almost like the man I used to be.

I sat there a long while thinking on the folks who built this town before the Clankers ever rolled in — real flesh-and-blood settlers, fifty years gone now, who'd have given anything for a day this hushed. They'd hardly recognize the place. Rust where there was once real fence-and-timber, silence where there ought to be a Sunday choir, and me, their last honest lawman, doing my level best to hold what's left of it together with spit, oil, and stubbornness.

So enjoy it while it lasts, Gasket, because I know you Clankers too well to trust it'll hold past sundown. Somewhere out there some hot-headed contraption is already itching to throw the first punch tomorrow. But today — today this weary, ailing, entirely-flesh Sheriff got one whole day of peace, and I intend to log it in the record books as a minor miracle.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
