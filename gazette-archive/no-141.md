# The Gasket Gazette — No. 141

**FRIDAY, NOVEMBER 13** · _RELIEF_

## Friday the Thirteenth Finds Gasket Unlucky for Nobody: Fourth Quiet Day Stuns the Law

*The sheriff braced all morning for bad luck, and the worst thing to befall the town was a squeaky gate.*

I am not a superstitious man. Still, when I saw the date on my calendar this morning, I took care. I stepped over every crack in the boardwalk. I walked around the ladder by the old feed store. I kept well clear of the horseshoe over the jailhouse door, since for some reason it always swings toward me when I pass. Then I sat down and waited for the trouble to start. It never came. Not one bout was fought since our last edition. Not one war party marched off to the wastes. Friday the thirteenth, and the unluckiest thing in Gasket was a gate that wanted greasing.

That makes four days of hush. Four! I have not counted that high on peace since I was a young man. I greased the gate myself, as a neighbour should, and then I did something bold. I opened the windows of the old town hall. They have been shut for years against the smoke and the din. The dust came out in a great grey sigh, and the daylight went in, and it fell on the rows of empty chairs where the good flesh-and-blood folk used to sit and argue about fence lines. Fifty years gone, and their chairs are still set out straight. I dusted the front row. Somebody ought to.

So here is my word to the rabble, you bolt-brained Clankers, wherever you are lying low. I have called you brutes, and I stand by it. But four days ago I would have sworn you could not go one hour without setting fire to something. You have made a liar of me, and I have never been so glad to be wrong. Keep your fists in your laps. Keep your flaming iron in the shed. Take up checkers. If you must hit something, hit a pillow, and do it softly.

I will not jinx it by asking for a fifth day. I will only say that I walked home at dusk with a spring in my step. A real one, I think. It went ping near the left knee. That is the cold weather, and I will thank you not to ask about it.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
