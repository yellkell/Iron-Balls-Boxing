# The Gasket Gazette — No. 117

**TUESDAY, OCTOBER 20** · _RELIEF_

## A HUSH FALLS OVER GASKET, AND I DON'T TRUST IT

*Not one fist thrown since the last edition, and the sheriff can hardly bear the happiness.*

Citizens, I have a confession to make. I sat on my porch this morning and heard nothing. No clang. No boom. No flaming iron ball whistling past my ear. Just wind, and a loose shutter, and the sweet creak of my own good flesh-and-blood knees. Not one bout since the last edition. Not one. If I were a man given to weeping, I would weep. (I am not. The moisture on my cheek is a leak. It's the weather.)

Naturally the rabble wasted no time spoiling the mood. A fresh arrival rolled into town, a unit calling itself IRON-B37F, rattling its bolts and looking around for trouble. Let the record show that so far it has found none, and I am watching that unit like a hawk watches a mouse. Welcome to Gasket. Keep your hands in your pockets and your fire in your boiler.

Fifty years ago this town was full of honest folk who knew how to be quiet. They packed up and left, and who can blame them, with the Clankers moving in. But today, for one blessed day, I got a taste of how it used to be. I want more of it. Lay down your fists, you magnificent nuisances. Let an old town rest. And somebody fetch me a quart of oil. Not for me, you understand. For the shutter.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
