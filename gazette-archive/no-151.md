# The Gasket Gazette — No. 151

**MONDAY, NOVEMBER 23** · _BLISS_

## Two Weeks Quiet: Sheriff Has Nothing to Do on a Monday and Loves Every Minute

*A full fourteen days without a bout or a war party, and the law spends its Monday morning writing up an empty jailhouse ledger.*

Two weeks. Fourteen days. A whole fortnight, if you want to sound fancy, and today I do. Since our last edition not one Clanker has raised a fist in Gasket, not one flaming iron ball has come crashing through the barbershop window, and not one posse has marched out past the town line to go poking the wild engines in the wastes. It is Monday morning, the day honest folk go back to work, and I am proud to report that I have no work to go back to.

I opened the jailhouse ledger at sunrise, licked my pencil, and wrote today's date at the top of a clean page. Then I sat there. Under ARRESTS, I wrote nothing. Under COMPLAINTS, nothing. Under DAMAGE TO TOWN PROPERTY, I wrote nothing so neatly you could frame it. I have never seen a prettier page in my life. I read it over three times, and each time my chest made a soft ticking sound, like a pocket watch. That is my heart. It is simply very happy, and very old.

It put me in mind of the old Gasket, fifty years back, when the flesh-and-blood families still lived here and a lawman's Monday was nothing but lost cats and a church social. I miss those folks every day. But I will say this: for two whole weeks this town has felt almost like theirs again.

So here is my Monday word to every creaking contraption in Gasket Territory, JAKETHEPRO and XYFUME, YOMAMAOKAY and JFIGHTER2 and every last one of your rattling crowd: fourteen days, and the town still stands. The ledger is blank, the streets are swept, and the sheriff has had a good night's sleep and a fresh quart of oil. Let us go for three weeks. I dare you. Peace looks good on you, even if you will never admit it.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
