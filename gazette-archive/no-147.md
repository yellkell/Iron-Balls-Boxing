# The Gasket Gazette — No. 147

**THURSDAY, NOVEMBER 19** · _BLISS_

## Ten Days of Peace: Something Sprouts in the Sheriff's Garden

*A tenth straight day without a bout or a war party, and the washer patch behind the general store shows its first sign of life. Sort of.*

Ten days. Double digits! I had to borrow a second hand to count it, and the only spare one in town was my own, lying on the jailhouse desk where it fell off Tuesday. Since our last edition not one Clanker has thrown a punch in Gasket. Not one flaming iron ball has rolled down Main Street. Not one posse has marched out past the town line to go bother the wild engines in the wastes. The silence is so thick you could spread it on toast.

I promised you news from the garden, and I keep my promises. This morning I walked out to Widow Pruitt's old tomato patch, where I planted those three rows of washers, and there it was: a little orange bloom poking up out of the dirt. Rust. The washers have rusted. Now, a cynic would call that a ruined pocketful of hardware. I call it the first thing to come up out of that ground in fifty years, ever since the Pruitts and every other honest flesh-and-blood family packed their wagons and left this town to the Clankers. I watered it. I will be watering it again tomorrow.

I did catch a chill out there, standing in the morning damp. My nose began to drip something dark and slick that smelled a great deal like engine oil, which the town doctor, if we still had one, would surely call a perfectly ordinary head cold. That cruel wasting sickness has turned another few inches of my good honest hide to plate steel this week, and some nights I lie awake listening to it tick. But not today. Today I am too happy to tick.

So here is my word to you, you dented, sputtering brotherhood of bolts. YELLKELL, REDWOLFWEB, JKING123, CRYSTALZACH, POO1, JFIGHTER, MAXMANGO, GODLYLUK3, YOMAMAOKAY, XYFUME, JAKETHEPRO, JFIGHTER2, and every last rattletrap in Gasket Territory: ten whole days, and not a single fist raised among you. I do not know who taught you manners, but if I find out I will pin a tin star on them. Keep your boilers cool, keep your hands in your pockets, and come see the garden. Quietly. Wipe your feet.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
