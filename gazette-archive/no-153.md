# The Gasket Gazette — No. 153

**WEDNESDAY, NOVEMBER 25** · _GRATITUDE_

## Sixteen Days of Silence: Sheriff Plans a Thanksgiving Feast for a Town of One

*Not a bout, not a brawl, not a single war party since last edition, and the law intends to give thanks with every last drop of gravy he owns.*

Sixteen days. I counted them again this morning just to be sure, scratching a tally on the jailhouse wall, and the number held. Sixteen days with no fists on Main Street, no flaming iron sailing over the feed store, and no war party stomping off to the wastes to go picking fights with mountains. Tomorrow is Thanksgiving, and for once this old town has something real to be thankful for: nothing happened. Gloriously, beautifully, nothing at all.

So I am throwing a feast. I dragged the long table out of the old meeting hall, the one the flesh-and-blood families used to fill every November, back when Gasket had pie on the windowsills and children in the street. I set it for forty out of habit. Fifty years now since the last of them rolled out of town, and I still put out the good plates. Then I went to the pantry for the gravy and found I had already drunk it straight from the can, and I must say it went down smoother than any gravy has a right to. Tasted a touch of motor oil, if I am honest. Must be a regional recipe.

Now, I am told some of you Clankers have been sitting quiet in your sheds. YELLKELL, JKING123, CRYSTALZACH, MAXMANGO, JFIGHTER and the rest of you rattling rabble: I see you. I see you not swinging. And while I would sooner eat a horseshoe than set a place for a contraption at my table, I will say this much: sixteen days of peace is the finest thing any of you have ever made. Keep making it.

So tomorrow, while you all sit in your sheds doing whatever it is metal folk do on a holiday, I will be at the head of that long table, saying grace over an empty room and a fresh can of gravy. And I will be thankful. Thankful for the quiet, thankful for the hush, and thankful that for one more day, not one of you lifted a fist in my town. Do not spoil my dessert.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
