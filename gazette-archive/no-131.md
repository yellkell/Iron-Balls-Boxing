# The Gasket Gazette — No. 131

**TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 3** · _RELIEF_

## NOT A SINGLE FIST FLIES IN GASKET

*A whole day of hush, and this old lawman nearly wept into his oil pan.*

Friends, neighbors, and whatever you Clankers count as neighbors: I have news so rare I had to read the wire twice. Nobody fought. Not one swing. Not one flaming iron ball hurled across my dusty street. The ring sat cold, the sky stayed clear, and the only sound in all of Gasket was the wind rattling a loose shutter.

I sat on the porch and listened to it for an hour. Fifty years ago the good, honest folk of this town knew silence like this every day, before they packed up and left us to the rust. I miss them. I miss their pie. I miss a street where nothing explodes. If you rowdy contraptions can manage one quiet day, you can manage two. I am asking nicely. I am asking on my knees, which, I admit, creak something terrible.

Mind you, I'm not saying I enjoyed it because I'm tired. A flesh-and-blood man of my stock does not get tired. I simply needed a lie-down and a quart of oil, and the hush was the finest tonic for it. Whatever that cursed wasting sickness is doing to my joints, even it seemed to go easy on me today. Let's do this again tomorrow, Clankers. Put the fists away and let an old town rest.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
