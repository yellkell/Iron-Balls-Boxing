# The Gasket Gazette — No. 140

**THURSDAY, NOVEMBER 12** · _GLEE_

## Three Days of Hush: Sheriff Fears He Has Gone Deaf, Finds He Has Merely Been Blessed

*The quiet in Gasket runs to a third straight day, and the law has started to hope against its better judgment.*

I woke this Thursday and heard nothing. No boom. No hiss of a flaming ball. No Clanker hollering about its own greatness. For one cold second I thought my ears had finally quit on me, so I rapped on the side of my head to check. It rang clear as a dinner bell, as any healthy skull will. My hearing is fine. The town is simply quiet. Not one bout was fought since our last edition, and not one war party went stomping off to the wastes. That makes three days running, and I have counted them twice.

I spent the morning doing something I have not dared in years. I hung a picture. It is a small painting of a cow, left behind in the old schoolhouse when the good flesh-and-blood folk rode out fifty years ago. Every time I nailed it up before, some iron fool shook it off the wall by suppertime. Today it hung straight from breakfast to dusk. I stood and looked at that cow for a good long while. It looked back at me with real warmth, which is more than I can say for my neighbours.

Now hear me, you rivet-headed rabble, wherever you are sulking. I do not know why you have set down your fists. Maybe you ran out of fire. Maybe you ran out of nerve. I do not care which. This is what a town is meant to sound like, and you are, at long last, doing it right. Keep doing it. Sit on your hands. Count the clouds. Learn a card game, a slow one. Peace is not hard. You just have to do nothing, and you have always been so good at nothing useful.

I will not call it a truce, for a Clanker's promise is worth less than a wet match. But I slept eight hours flat on my back last night and did not seize up even once. I mean to try for nine.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
