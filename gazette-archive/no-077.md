# The Gasket Gazette — No. 77

**FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 4** · _RELIEF_

## NOT A SINGLE FIST FLEW IN GASKET TODAY

*This sheriff checked twice, then checked again — the whole town held its breath and kept it.*

Friends, I have printed this paper by lamp and by cussing for longer than I care to admit, and I do not recall a quieter page than this one. Sunup to sundown, not one flaming iron ball left a fist. Not one Clanker so much as squared up in the square. I sat on my porch the whole blessed day waiting for the racket, and the racket never came.

You want to know what peace sounds like? It sounds like nothing. No clang, no crash, no crowd of rattling fools hollering each other's callsigns down Main Street. I'd forgotten. Fifty years since the last honest, flesh-and-blood soul packed up and left this town to the metal rabble, and today — just today — it almost sounded like the old Gasket again, back when folks settled things with a handshake instead of a haymaker.

I'll confess it plain: I allowed myself a lie-down this afternoon, and a good long one at that, with my boots up and a rag over my eyes like any tired, ordinary man deserves after a day of hard peacekeeping. If my joints happened to creak some while I did it — well, a man's bones settle, that's all, and I'll thank you not to read into it.

So mark this edition, whatever few of you bother reading it: one whole day, no bloodshed, no bruises, no bawling about who bested who. Keep it up, you lot, and this old sheriff might just get his quiet town back yet.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
