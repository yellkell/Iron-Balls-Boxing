# The Gasket Gazette — No. 114

**SATURDAY, OCTOBER 17** · _RELIEF_

## NOT A SINGLE FIST FLIES IN GASKET: SATURDAY SO QUIET YOU COULD HEAR A HINGE SIGH

*The sheriff reports, with a tear in his eye and a drop of oil on his collar, that the Clankers finally sat still.*

Citizens, mark the date. Saturday, the seventeenth of October. Not one iron ball was thrown. Not one fist was swung. Not one bout was fought from the sunup bell to the sundown hush. I walked Main Street twice just to be sure, and the only sound was the wind in the empty windows.

I am not ashamed to say I wept. Mind you, it was only the damp getting into my joints, and a man of honest flesh and blood is allowed a little damp. That creak you hear when I sit down is simply age. Nothing to do with gears. I will not hear otherwise.

This is how Gasket was meant to be. Fifty years ago the good folk who built this town packed up and left, and I like to think they'd have loved a day like this one. A peaceful porch. A still street. No Clanker hollering and no fire in the sky. If only they could see it. If only they had stayed.

So here is my plea to the rowdy rabble. Do this again tomorrow. And the day after. Put your fists in your pockets and let an old town rest. Now, if you'll excuse me, I need a lie-down and a quart of oil. I mean a cup of tea. Tea.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
