# The Gasket Gazette — No. 135

**SATURDAY, NOVEMBER 7** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF SILENCE: GASKET HEARS ITS OWN HINGES

*Not one fist, not one flaming ball, and this old sheriff may have wept into his oil pan.*

Citizens, I have to sit down. Well, I'd sit, if my knees would bend without that awful squeal. Today, not a single Clanker threw a punch in Gasket. No bouts. No raids out in the wastes. No iron balls whistling over the rooftops. I checked the wire twice, then a third time, just to be sure it wasn't a trick.

Do you know what a quiet Saturday sounds like? Neither did I, friends. I had nearly forgotten. There was wind in the empty windows. There was a loose shutter tapping out its slow little song. There was a faint ticking from somewhere in my chest, which is surely my good honest pocket watch and nothing else. The monsters out in the wastes slept undisturbed, and so did I. Mostly. Between you and me, I did need a lie-down and a quart of oil afterward, the relief was that heavy.

It makes a man think of the old days, when real flesh-and-blood folk filled these streets and a weekend meant pie, not pistons. Fifty years they've been gone, and every dull day since I have wished them back. Today, for once, the town felt almost like theirs again. Let this be a lesson to you, you rowdy rabble: peace is possible, and it costs you nothing but sitting still. I know it's hard for you. Try it again tomorrow.

I'll be on my porch, listening to the hush. Nobody touch it.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
