# The Gasket Gazette — No. 98

**THURSDAY, OCTOBER 1** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF HUSH: NOT ONE FIST FLIES IN GASKET

*The sheriff reports, with a tear in his optic, that the whole town got through Thursday without a single bang.*

Friends, neighbours, and assorted scrap: I have to sit down. Not one bout. Not one iron ball thrown. Not one raid marched out past the town line to pick a fight with a mountain. For a full day, Gasket was quiet, and I heard the wind in the old rafters for the first time in longer than I care to say.

I walked Main Street at noon and listened to nothing at all. It was glorious. A tumbleweed rolled past and did not even squeak. I stood there a good long while, and I will admit my joints ached from standing so still, and I may need a lie-down and a quart of oil. But that is just the dry air. A man of my stock gets stiff in the knees. It is the wasting sickness too, I fear, the cruel creeping of cold rivets where good honest flesh used to be. I will not speak of it further.

Mark this day, Clankers. This is what peace looks like. It is not hard. You simply put your fists in your pockets and let a tired old town rest. Fifty years ago the last of the real folk packed up and left, and I like to think a day like this is the sort they dreamed of when they were still here. Do it again tomorrow. I am begging. I will even say please, which I do not say lightly.

This column has no agitators to name today, and what a joy that is to write. Long may the hush hold.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
