# The Gasket Gazette — No. 96

**TUESDAY, SEPTEMBER 29** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF HUSH: NOT ONE FIST FLIES IN GASKET

*The sheriff reports, with a catch in his throat, that the town has finally heard itself think.*

Friends, neighbours, and whatever else is out there: I write this with a tear in my eye and a drop of something wet leaking from a joint. Not a single fist flew in Gasket today. Not one iron ball was thrown. Not one Clanker rolled up the main street bellowing for a rematch. No raiding party marched out to pick a fight with a monster, either. The whole territory is quiet, and I am nearly overcome.

Listen to it. That is the sound of a town at rest. The wind through the empty windows. A shutter tapping in the breeze. The slow tick of my own boiler, which I have decided is a healthy old heart and will hear no argument otherwise. For one glorious day the rabble kept its fists in its pockets, and I did not have to name a single agitator. I will not spoil it by naming any now.

Fifty years ago, when the last of the good flesh-and-blood folk packed their wagons and left, this is the kind of quiet they meant to leave behind. They didn't mean the racket the Clankers made of it. Today, for once, the peace matched the memory. I sat on the porch and let the rust settle, and I nearly wept. Oil, I mean. Nearly wept oil. A figure of speech.

I ask only this: do it again tomorrow. Sit down. Lay your fists on the table. Let an old lawman have a second quiet day, and perhaps a third. My joints ache for it, and so does the whole town.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
