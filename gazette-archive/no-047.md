# The Gasket Gazette — No. 47

**WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 5** · _EXASPERATION_

## EIGHT NEW GEARHEADS ROLL INTO TOWN AS TWO OLD ONES WON'T QUIT SWINGING

*Sheriff Cole Ironside counts the new faces crowding a town that used to belong to somebody else.*

Gasket had itself another restless Wednesday, and I laid the blame where it belongs: on YK and JFIGHTER2, who between the two of them kept my quiet street ringing like a dropped toolbox. YK threw the most punches of anybody wearing a callsign this week, and JFIGHTER2 wasn't far behind, both of them apparently under the impression that a frontier town is a punching bag with a zip code. It is not. It is a town. Or it was, before folks like you got hold of it.

And as if two brawlers weren't enough noise for one dusty street, eight new contraptions came clattering in through the gate this week and set up shop like they owned the place. IRON-0057. IRON-DB36. POO1, of all the names to paint on a mailbox. I don't know where they're manufactured, but I wish they'd manufacture a little less of themselves right here in Gasket. Fifty years ago this town had real neighbors — flesh, blood, front porches, the works — and now I'm out here counting serial numbers like a fella at a scrapyard auction.

I'll admit my joints ache something fierce writing all this down tonight, and a man's boiler can only take so much clanging before it wants a lie-down. Not that I'd know a thing about boilers personally, mind you — just an expression, same as any honest frontier lawman might use of an evening.

All I ask, same as I've asked every week since this town started rusting at the edges, is for one single day where nobody swings a fist. Is that so much? Put the iron down. Let an old town breathe. That's all Cole Ironside's got to say about it tonight.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
