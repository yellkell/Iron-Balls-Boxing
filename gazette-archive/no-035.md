# The Gasket Gazette — No. 35

**FRIDAY, JULY 24** · _DREAD_

## THIRD QUIET SUNDOWN IN GASKET — AND SEVEN FRESH CLANKERS JUST ROLLED THROUGH THE GATE

*Sheriff Ironside counts three blessed days without a single punch thrown, and wonders why that never feels like good news.*

Three days now. Three whole sundowns and not one fireball, not one holler, not one dented plate dragged into my office crying foul. A man could get used to a town this quiet. I nearly did, right up until I heard the gate creak seven times in one afternoon and about swallowed my own badge.

Seven of them. Not one, not three like we've seen before — seven brand-new units came rattling into Gasket today, fresh rivets and paint you could still smell: WYATT, IRON-EE1A, IRON-E58C, IRON-88C8, IRON-844F, IRON-4FA0, and IRON-3AAA. Not one has thrown a punch yet, and I'll grant them that much credit while it lasts. But a trickle of newcomers is one thing. Seven arriving the same day a town's gone quiet is not a coincidence, it's a storm gathering just past the ridge. Whatever's coming, Gasket, brace for it.

I'll confess this waiting does something to a man's constitution. My hands have gone stiff as fence wire the last few nights, and there's a cold, creeping ache working through my joints that no doctor in this territory can name — some wasting business turning good honest bone to gear and rivet, little by little, against my will. I did not ask for this affliction and I will not accept it kindly. But I notice it goes quiet exactly when the town does, and that's the only mercy I've been offered lately.

Still, I'll take three hushed days over none, same as I'd have taken the two before it, same as I keep hoping this whole rusted town might one day settle the way it did fifty years back, before the last of the flesh-and-blood folk packed their wagons and left it to the rest of us. Seven new faces are watching now, and so am I. Behave yourselves, the lot of you, or I'll be back tomorrow with a considerably shorter fuse.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
