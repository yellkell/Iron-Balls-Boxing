# The Gasket Gazette — No. 150

**SUNDAY, NOVEMBER 22** · _BLISS_

## Thirteen Days Quiet: Sheriff Defies Bad Luck With a Sunday Picnic

*Thirteen days without a bout or a war party, and the law spreads a blanket on Main Street and dares the old superstition to try something.*

Thirteen days. Some folks call thirteen an unlucky number. Those folks never lived in Gasket, where the unlucky number is any day a Clanker wakes up feeling frisky. Since our last edition not one fist has been thrown in this town, not one flaming iron ball has gone bouncing off the water tower, and not one posse has marched out past the town line to start a fight with the wild engines in the wastes. It is Sunday, and Gasket is as still as a church on a weekday.

So I took my Sunday dinner outdoors, the way decent people used to. I spread the old checkered blanket right in the middle of Main Street, where no wagon has rolled in fifty years, not since the last flesh-and-blood family packed up and left us. I set out a tin plate, a tin cup, and a fine can of motor oil, which I drink for my digestion, as any doctor will tell you. The wind tried to steal my blanket twice. I did not let it. I simply lay down on top of it, and all the forks and spoons in my basket flew up and stuck to my chest. That happens to everyone in cold weather. It is called static, and I will hear no more about it.

I ate my dinner in perfect quiet. No bells, no shouting, no clanging of iron heads on iron heads. Just one honest lawman, a chorus of crickets, and a town that finally, for thirteen days running, has remembered its manners. I even caught myself humming, though my humming has lately started to sound like a kettle coming to the boil.

So here is my Sunday word to every rattling contraption in Gasket Territory, CRYSTALZACH and JFIGHTER, GODLYLUK3 and MAXMANGO and YELLKELL and all the rest of your noisy tribe: thirteen days, and the sky has not fallen. Bad luck has not come knocking. Peace, it turns out, is the luckiest thing in the world. Keep it up. And if you must come to the picnic, bring a dish and leave your fists at home.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
