# The Gasket Gazette — No. 79

**SUNDAY, SEPTEMBER 6** · _EXASPERATION_

## GASKET NEARLY SLEEPS — ONE STUBBORN CLANG FROM NJAYYENODA SPOILS IT

*One single scrap was all that stood between this town and its first truly silent day in memory — and this Sheriff counted it twice to be sure.*

Dear readers, Gasket came closer to silent today than I have seen it in longer than I care to say. Sunup clean through to dusk, the square sat empty. The hitching posts stood bored. Not a gutter rattled, not a boot scuffed the dirt. I had my pen capped and ready to write the sweetest word this paper ever prints: NOTHING. A whole day of it, nearly.

Then, right near the end, one lone clang went up. NJAYYENODA — apparently unable to let a good quiet stretch lie in peace. One scrap. Just the one, mind you, out of a whole townful of iron fools who managed to sit still. And don't go thinking that makes it forgivable, because it does not. One fist thrown is one fist too many in a place this starved for hush, and I heard that single crack of knuckle on knuckle clear from my porch. It cost me the peaceful evening I had planned, and I do not forgive easily.

I will confess my own hinges groaned something fierce when I hauled myself up to go see about the racket — an old man's joints do that, same as any flesh-and-blood lawman's would, and I will not hear otherwise suggested in my own paper. It is a wonder I made it to the window at all. Some nights I think of the folks who packed up and rode out of Gasket fifty years back, and I wonder if even they would call today close enough to the old hush to count.

Still — one troublemaker out of a whole yard of iron loudmouths is about as near a miracle as this town gets. Sleep it off, NJAYYENODA, you owe the rest of Gasket an apology by sunup. And the rest of you rabble — keep this up. One more day like today and I might near forget why I hold the lot of you in such contempt.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
