# The Gasket Gazette — No. 44

**SUNDAY, AUGUST 2** · _WARINESS_

## ONE PUNCH BROKE THE HUSH, AND FOUR NEW SHADOWS ROLLED IN BEHIND IT

*Sheriff Cole Ironside on the quietest day Gasket's had in a while — and why he still can't rest easy.*

For most of yesterday, dear reader, I near forgot what my own porch sounded like without a fist connecting with something. Near total hush. I'd gone so far as to pour myself a cup of coffee and sit a spell, same as a real, flesh-and-blood lawman might've done fifty years back, before this town went to rust and rabble. Then, sure as sunset, one contraption couldn't help itself. IRON-9969 went and threw down anyway — a single scrap, one solitary disturbance, but enough to remind me that peace in Gasket is never a promise, only a lucky recess.

I won't pretend it ruined my whole day. One brawl is a mercy compared to the usual iron thunder that shakes the shutters off this town. But I'll be hanged if my hinges didn't seize right up when I heard the racket start — my knees, I mean, my good honest knees, aching the way any decent man's knees ache after a long week standing sentry. Don't go reading anything metal into that.

What's got me pacing the boardwalk tonight, though, ain't the one punch. It's the four new faces that came creaking into town same as the dust rolled in behind them: GODLYLUK3, IRON-C057, IRON-59B9, and IRON-B7FA, none of 'em thrown a blow yet far as the wire tells me, all of 'em standing at the edge of Gasket like they're sizing up the place. I've seen that quiet before. It never stays quiet.

So I'll take my one small mercy and be grateful for it, the way the old-timers used to be grateful for rain — but I'm keeping my good eye on them four newcomers. This town buried its manners fifty years ago when the last decent folk packed up the wagon and left it to us. I'd hate to watch it bury what little hush it's got left, too.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
