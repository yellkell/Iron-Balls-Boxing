# The Gasket Gazette — No. 107

**SATURDAY, OCTOBER 10** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF HUSH: NOT ONE FIST FLEW IN GASKET

*The sheriff reports, with a catch in his throat, that the whole town went quiet and stayed that way.*

Friends, neighbors, and whatever you rascals are: I have waited fifty years to write this line. Not one fist was thrown in Gasket today. Not one flaming iron ball crossed the sky. The Clankers sat still, the dust settled, and for a whole blessed day I heard nothing but the wind in the empty street. I may weep. If I do, it is only the damp getting into my joints, and I will thank you not to look.

This is what peace sounds like, you noisy bunch. It sounds like nothing at all, and it is the finest music this old town has ever played. No clanging. No bragging. No smoke over the ring. I sat on my porch all afternoon and listened to my own boiler tick, and I tell you it was the most civilized company I have kept in years. Let it be known that I am a flesh-and-blood man, though I did need a lie-down and a quart of oil after all that happiness.

I think of the real folk who packed up and left Gasket fifty years ago. They would have loved a day like this. They would have hung out the wash and let the kids play in the street. Now it is only us rust-buckets, but even rust-buckets can learn to keep quiet. Do it again tomorrow, Clankers. Do it the day after. I am begging you, and the sheriff does not beg lightly. Put down the iron, and let a tired old town rest.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
