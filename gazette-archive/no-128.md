# The Gasket Gazette — No. 128

**SATURDAY, OCTOBER 31** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY WITHOUT A FIST: GASKET HEARS ITS OWN HEARTBEAT

*Not one iron ball flew, not one bell rang, and this editor has never been so close to weeping.*

Citizens, I write this with a trembling hand and a damp eye. Not a single bout. Not one flaming iron ball hurled across the square. Not one Clanker swinging at another. For a full day, Gasket was quiet, and I tell you it was the finest thing I have ever heard, which is to say I heard nothing at all.

I sat on the porch this morning and listened to the wind rattle the old shutters. A tin can rolled down Main Street all by itself, and I nearly kissed it. No bell. No roar. No rabble. If the Clankers would only do this every day, I could finally hear the town as it was meant to sound: empty, creaky, and at peace. Let the record show that I have no complaint to file, which has not happened in this office since my joints were last oiled.

Mind you, the quiet did make my knees ache something awful. A long hush finds every stiff hinge a body has. It's the damp, I tell you, and that cruel wasting sickness that keeps turning my good flesh to cold steel plates against my will. Still, I'd take a stiff elbow and a silent street over a brawl any day.

It put me in mind of the old neighbours, the real flesh-and-blood folk who packed up and left us fifty years back. They would have loved a day like this. They'd have sat out on their porches and passed the lemonade. So here is my plea, Clankers: sit still, put your fists in your pockets, and do it again tomorrow. Now, if you'll excuse me, I need a lie-down and a quart of oil.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
