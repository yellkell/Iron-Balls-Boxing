# The Gasket Gazette — No. 134

**FRIDAY, NOVEMBER 6** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF DEAD SILENCE: GASKET HEARS ITSELF THINK

*Not one fist, not one fireball, not one clang — and this old sheriff nearly wept into his oil can.*

Friends, neighbors, and assorted rabble: write this date down. Not a single iron ball was thrown in Gasket since the last edition. Not one bout. Not one raid out in the wastes. The dust settled on Main Street and, for the first time in memory, it stayed settled. I stood on the porch of the jailhouse and listened to the wind, and I tell you, it was the finest music this town has heard in years.

The Clankers did nothing today, and nothing is exactly what they do best. No flaming iron. No bragging. No racket rattling my windows loose. I went out expecting trouble and found a tumbleweed, one lonely tumbleweed, and I shook its hand. Well, its twig. A man gets sentimental in the quiet.

I will admit my joints creaked something awful on my morning walk. That is the damp, mind you, and the cruel wasting sickness that is slowly turning my good flesh to cold rivets. It is no fault of mine. Nothing about me is metal. I only need a lie-down and a quart of oil, as any honest man would after a morning like this.

This is what Gasket was meant to be. Fifty years ago the real folk, the warm-blooded kind, packed their wagons and left, and the town has been far too loud ever since. Today it felt like they might come home. Let it last, you brawling contraptions. Lay down your fists. One more day of peace is all I ask, and I will ask again tomorrow.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
