# The Gasket Gazette — No. 105

**THURSDAY, OCTOBER 8** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF HUSH: NOT ONE FIST LIFTED IN GASKET

*The sheriff reports, with a wet eye and a squeaky hinge, that the town slept in peace.*

Friends, neighbors, and assorted scrap: I have waited a long time to write these words. Nobody fought. Not a ball was thrown. Not a spark was struck. The streets of Gasket lay still as a church pew, and I sat on my porch and listened to the wind rattle the old shutters like a lullaby. If this is what peace sounds like, I want to be buried in it.

Let the record show that the Clankers, those clanging, swaggering, ball-hurling contraptions, managed one whole day without wrecking the place. I do not praise them. I merely note it, the way you note a rainbow after a flood. Let them ask themselves how it felt. Let them ask whether the quiet was so terrible. I suspect it was not. I suspect even they enjoyed it.

My joints sang along, I confess. A good hush does wonders for a man's boiler, and I felt twenty years younger, which is to say only ninety. I shall now take a short lie-down and a quart of oil, purely for my flesh, which is entirely flesh, whatever that creaking noise may suggest. It is only the illness, you understand, the cruel wasting sickness that is turning my honest muscle to cold rivets. Nothing more.

Fifty years ago this town was full of real folk, flesh and blood, who knew how to enjoy a quiet evening. I like to think they would have approved of today. So I ask you, Gasket, for one more day like it. Keep your fists down and let an old town rest.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
