# The Gasket Gazette — No. 144

**MONDAY, NOVEMBER 16** · _JOY_

## Seven Days of Peace: Sheriff Rings the Chapel Bell, Loses a Shoulder Bolt

*A full week without one bout or a single war party, and the law keeps a promise it never thought it would have to keep.*

Seven days. A whole week. I said it could not be done, and then I said if it were done I would ring the bell. Well, I am a man of my word, and this is a Monday I will remember. Since our last edition not one Clanker has thrown a punch in Gasket. Not one ball of fire has scorched the boardwalk. Not one posse has gone stomping out to bother the wild engines in the wastes. Seven quiet sunrises in a row. I counted every one of them twice.

So at first light I climbed back up to the old chapel on the hill and took hold of that rope. I pulled it once for the fiddle players. I pulled it again for the pie ladies. I pulled it a third time for every honest flesh-and-blood family that loaded up its wagon and rolled out of here fifty years ago, back when this town still had curtains in the windows and children on the porches. The bell rang out clear across the empty streets. I hope, wherever those good folks ended up, some faint echo of it found them.

On the fourth pull something in my shoulder went PING and a bolt rolled down the chapel steps. I am told by nobody at all that this is the sickness again, that cruel wasting that turns a man's good warm meat into nuts and washers one joint at a time. I picked the bolt up, screwed it back in, and finished my ringing. A lesser man would have needed a wrench. I needed only a wrench and a quart of oil, and I will thank you not to remark on it.

Now listen close, you clattering tin-headed Clankers. YELLKELL, REDWOLFWEB, JKING123, CRYSTALZACH, POO1, JFIGHTER, and every last rattletrap that has ever kept me up at night: you did it. A full week, and not one of you lifted a fist. I will not pretend I trust it. I still sleep with my star pinned on. But for one bright morning this old town sounded like a town again instead of a junkyard, and that is your doing as much as mine. Keep your boilers cold and your hands in your pockets. Give me an eighth day, and I might just ring that bell again.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
