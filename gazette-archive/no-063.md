# The Gasket Gazette — No. 63

**FRIDAY, AUGUST 21** · _RELIEF_

## NOT ONE FIST FLEW LAST NIGHT, AND I SLEPT LIKE A BABY

*For one blessed stretch, Gasket held its breath and nobody swung it.*

Friends, I have been sheriffing this dust-heap of a town for more years than I care to count on these creaky old fingers, and I tell you true: last night not a single Clanker threw a punch. Not one. No flaming iron ball crossed this town's air, no war-whoop rattled a single loose shingle. I sat on my porch in the quiet and, for a spell, I almost remembered what Gasket sounded like before it went to the rabble.

Fifty years now since the last of the good, honest, flesh-and-blood folk packed their wagons and left this place to the rust and the racket. I still hear the echo of their footsteps some nights, back when a man could sit on his porch without a fireball whistling past his hat. Last night I could almost pretend they never left at all. Almost.

Don't mistake me — I know this hush won't hold. Somewhere out there a Clanker is oiling its knuckles this very minute, saving up its mischief for tomorrow's edition. But for one night, this town rested, and so, for once, did these old bones of mine — creaky joints and all, on account of the damp, mind you, nothing more sinister than that. A man's allowed a quiet ache now and then. Savor it while it lasts, Gasket. I know I will.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
