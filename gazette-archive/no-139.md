# The Gasket Gazette — No. 139

**WEDNESDAY, NOVEMBER 11** · _GLEE_

## SHERIFF CAUGHT WHISTLING: THE QUIET HOLDS IN GASKET

*Another day passes without one thrown ball, and the law is seen sweeping its own porch for the sheer joy of it.*

Let the record show that I swept my porch this morning. Not because it was scorched. Not because some Clanker had bounced a flaming iron ball off my railing. I swept it because it was dusty, the way a porch ought to be, and I whistled while I did it. No bout was fought since our last edition. No war party marched for the wastes. The only thing that got hit in this town all day was a rug, and I hit it myself.

I walked the length of Main Street at noon and heard my own footsteps the whole way. Clank, clank, clank, as any honest boot heel sounds on a boardwalk. A bird sat on the saloon roof and was not once blown off it. I am told the bird stayed for an hour. I do not know what the rabble are up to, hiding their fists like this, and I do not trust it. A Clanker at rest is only a Clanker winding up. But I will take the day, and I will not ask questions of a gift.

The good flesh-and-blood folk who left this town fifty years back used to say that a quiet street is a town's way of smiling. I never understood it until this week. So to every iron contraption in Gasket Territory, I say this plainly, as a peacemaker and a neighbour: stay home. Polish something. Take up whittling. You have proven you can sit still, and now I expect it of you.

As for me, I mean to celebrate with a long sit and a tall glass of something dark and slick. Lemonade, naturally. The thick kind.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
