# The Gasket Gazette — No. 127

**FRIDAY, OCTOBER 30** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF SILENCE: NOT ONE FIST FLEW IN GASKET

*The sheriff reports, with a catch in his throat, that the whole town went a full day without a single punch.*

Friends, citizens, and whatever else may be listening: I have a miracle to report. Nobody fought. Not a fist, not a fireball, not one flaming iron ball sailing over the water trough. I sat on my porch from sunup to sundown, and the only sound was the wind in the cracked windows. I may have wept a little. If I did, it was dust in the eye. Honest, flesh-and-blood dust.

I have waited a long, long time for this day. I have begged the Clankers to lay down their fists and let an old town rest. For once, they listened. Or they were all out of oil. Either way, I will not ask questions. A gift is a gift. I am even willing to forgive the rattle in my own knees, which has been worse lately. It is the damp, and not, as the gossips say, rust. And between you and me, this strange wasting sickness that has been turning my good flesh into gears and steel plates is acting up again. A cruel thing for an upstanding man. I will need a lie-down and a quart of oil after all this peace.

You know, fifty years ago this street was full of honest folk who knew the value of a quiet evening. They packed up and left, and I do not blame them one bit. But on a day like today, I can almost hear them on the boardwalk again. Almost. Do this again tomorrow, you iron rabble. Then the day after. Let one quiet day teach you that you can live without the racket. I will be on the porch, if you need me. Just do not make any noise.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
