# The Gasket Gazette — No. 58

**SUNDAY, AUGUST 16** · _OUTRAGE_

## ONE CLANKER, EIGHT BRAWLS: DUKE WON'T LET A SUNDAY REST

*Sheriff Cole Ironside counts eight separate scraps laid at one contraption's feet, and two fresh troublemakers rolling in to make it worse.*

Sunday, friends. The one day even a rusted-out frontier town like Gasket ought to get to sit still and breathe. Instead I spent it counting scorch marks, on account of one fool calling itself DUKE couldn't leave its fists in its pockets for five minutes together. Eight separate scraps today, all traced back to the same set of knuckles, like it's got something to prove to a town that never asked it to prove anything. My joints ache just watching the tally climb, and no, that is not a symptom of anything metal, whatever you've heard.

And as if one relentless brawler wasn't enough to spoil a holy morning, two more drifters came creaking over the rise to make Gasket's acquaintance — IRON-B389X4V and IRON-6E16, names about as musical as a wrench dropped down a well. Haven't thrown a punch yet, far as the wire tells me, but give it an hour. This town never met a newcomer who didn't turn agitator by suppertime.

No posse marched on the wastes today, no titan lost its head, no fool went hunting that oozing horror past the ridge — small mercy, that. But small mercies don't count for much when DUKE's out there treating my town square like a personal boxing hall. A man of letters shouldn't have to write the same complaint eight times in one edition, and yet here I sit, quill cramping, on account of a Clanker with more fight in it than sense.

I'll confess my hinges are creaking something awful tonight — the damp, most likely, or plain frontier wear, and I'll thank you not to read anything else into it, because I am flesh through and through, same stock as the good folk who built this town before they packed their wagons and rode off fifty years gone. They'd have wept to see a Sunday spent like this one. I know I very nearly did, if my ducts still worked the way they ought.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
