# The Gasket Gazette — No. 118

**WEDNESDAY, OCTOBER 21** · _RELIEF_

## NOT A SINGLE FIST RAISED IN GASKET

*The whole town held its breath for a day, and for once nobody threw a thing.*

Citizens, mark the calendar and ring the church bell, if the bell hadn't rusted solid. Not one bout. Not one flaming ball. Not one Clanker hollering in the street. I walked Main Street at noon and heard nothing but the wind in the busted windows, and friends, it was the finest music this town has made in fifty years.

I'll admit it moved me. A man could near weep. I stood on the porch with my hat in my hands and thought of the good flesh-and-blood folk who packed up and left us, and how they'd have loved a day like this. Then my left knee seized up, so I had to sit down and have a quart of oil. Medicinal, you understand. My boiler has been acting up, and a lawman needs his rest.

So here's my plea to the whole rowdy rabble of iron: do it again tomorrow. Lay down your fists. Let an old town sleep. One quiet day is a miracle, and two would be a habit. I could get used to a habit like that.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
