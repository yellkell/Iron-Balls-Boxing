# The Gasket Gazette — No. 138

**TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 10** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY WITHOUT A SINGLE FLAMING BALL

*Gasket holds its breath, and for once nobody has set it on fire.*

Citizens, I write this morning with a tear in my eye and, I'll admit, a little grit in it too. Not one Clanker threw a single iron ball in anger since our last edition. No brawl, no raid, no racket. The streets lay still as a Sunday graveyard, and I have not enjoyed a quiet like it since the day the last good folk packed their wagons.

Fifty years ago, the flesh-and-blood neighbours of this town sat on their porches of an evening and listened to exactly this hush. Then they left, and the rabble rolled in with their swagger and their fire. Today the hush came back, just for a day, and I stood in the square and let it wash over me. My joints only creaked twice. That's the damp, mind you, and nothing else.

Don't take this for victory. The Clankers are merely resting, the way a boiler rests before it blows. But I'll sign my name to this one small mercy, and then go find a quart of oil and a lie-down. For my flesh, you understand. It's been aching something terrible, and I swear it feels a touch more like cold rivets every morning.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
