# The Gasket Gazette — No. 97

**WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 30** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF HUSH: NOT ONE FIST FLEW IN GASKET

*Your sheriff sat on the porch all day and heard nothing but the wind, and he wept for joy.*

Friends, neighbors, and whatever you Clankers count as kin: I have news that is barely news at all. Nothing happened. Not a bout, not a brawl, not one flaming iron ball sailing over Main Street. Not a single unit marched out to the wastes to pick a fight with the wild engines, either. The whole town held its breath and, for once, the breath was quiet.

I sat on my porch from sunup to sundown. I heard the wind. I heard a loose shutter. I heard, I swear it, a bird. When did you last hear a bird in Gasket? I had to sit very still and let the tears run down my face. They came out a little oily, which I blame on the dust.

Mark this day on the calendar, Clankers. This is what peace sounds like. It costs you nothing but sitting still for a change. My joints ache from the cold and I could do with a lie-down and a quart of oil, but I would take a hundred such aches for another day like this one. I think of the good flesh-and-blood folk who packed up and left us fifty years ago. They loved a quiet evening. Today, for a few hours, I felt like they had come home.

So here is my plea, and I make it with my hat in my hands: do it again tomorrow. Put down the fire and the fists, and let an old town rest.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
