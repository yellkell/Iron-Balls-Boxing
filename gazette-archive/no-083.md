# The Gasket Gazette — No. 83

**THURSDAY, SEPTEMBER 10** · _RELIEF_

## GASKET GOES SILENT — NOT ONE FIST FLEW ALL DAY LONG

*Sheriff Cole Ironside sits on his porch, hears nothing, and dares not trust it.*

Write it down, Gasket, because it may never happen again: not one iron ball left a single hand today. Not one clang, not one holler, not one Clanker come stomping past my window looking for a fight. I sat out on the porch from first light to last and heard nothing but wind in the gutters and my own old bones settling. A man could cry, if a man still had the plumbing for it.

No new faces rattled into town either, which suits me fine. Fifty years of watching strangers roll in and pick up the habit inside a week has taught me not to go looking for trouble on a good day. So I won't name a single troublemaker in this edition, because for once there isn't one. Let that sink in. Let it sit there next to the quiet, where it belongs.

I'll admit the hush does something for these old joints of mine — eases the ache some cruel affliction's been working into what used to be good honest flesh, turning it colder and harder by the week, though I'll not dwell on it further than that. A quiet day is the closest thing to medicine this town's got left.

So enjoy it while it lasts, because it never does. Somewhere out there a rattling fool is already itching to ruin my afternoon, and tomorrow's edition will find them soon enough. Today, though — today Gasket was almost the town it used to be, back when the folks here were flesh and blood and knew how to leave a man in peace.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
