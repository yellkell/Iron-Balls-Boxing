# The Gasket Gazette — No. 54

**WEDNESDAY, AUGUST 12** · _WARY_

## NOT ONE PUNCH THROWN — BUT TWO NEW CLANKERS SLINK INTO TOWN

*Sheriff Cole Ironside marks a blessed, silent day in Gasket, and eyes the newcomers like a man counting the matches before the fire starts.*

Let the record show it plain, because I intend to frame this edition and hang it over the jailhouse door: not a single iron ball flew in Gasket since my last report. Not one. No fists, no fire, no racket rattling the shutters. For one whole stretch of daylight this rusted-out husk of a town got to sit in the quiet the way it deserves, and I will not apologize for the tear in my eye over it. A man's joints ache less on a day like this. I mean that as a figure of speech, understand. Purely a figure of speech.

But peace in Gasket never comes without a catch, and today's catch rolled in on two sets of unfamiliar treads. BOONSON-BEN and IRON-4305 — names I'd never heard before this morning — have taken up residence in town, and I'll tell you what a fifty-year lawman's gut tells him about that: nothing good. New Clankers don't wander into Gasket to admire the scenery. They come to eventually throw a punch, same as all the rest of them, and I intend to watch both of you the way a man watches a kettle he knows is fixing to boil.

Still — credit where it's due, and it pains me to give it — neither of you has swung at a blessed thing yet. Keep it that way. This town buried its good, honest, flesh-and-blood folk's way of life fifty years back and has been nothing but a rattling scrapheap of grudges ever since; a quiet day like today is the closest thing to the old Gasket I remember, and I'd sooner not see it spoiled by two fresh faces looking to make a name for themselves with their knuckles.

So enjoy the hush while it lasts, town. I intend to. A man needs his rest — mine, anyhow, on account of a certain affliction the doctors still can't rightly explain, turning good honest flesh to gears and rivets against my will. Terrible business. Nothing whatsoever to do with the rust I scrub off my collar every morning. Nothing at all.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
