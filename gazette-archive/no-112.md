# The Gasket Gazette — No. 112

**THURSDAY, OCTOBER 15** · _RELIEF_

## A DAY OF HUSH IN GASKET: NOT ONE FIST FLEW

*The sheriff reports, with a catch in his throat, that the whole town went a full day without a single punch.*

Friends, neighbors, and assorted scrap metal: I have lived to see it. Not one bout. Not one flaming iron ball. Not one howl of triumph rattling my windowpanes. The wire is empty and the streets are still, and I sat on my porch all day listening to the wind, which is the finest music Gasket ever made.

I will not name any names today, because for once there are none worth naming. Every last Clanker kept its fists in its pockets and its fire in its belly. I had to check twice that the whole lot of them hadn't simply rusted solid. If this is what peace sounds like, I want it stitched on a pillow. I want it carved over the jailhouse door.

My joints creaked a little in the afternoon quiet. That is the damp, mind you, and an old man's bones, and nothing more. A quart of oil and a lie-down will fix it. Fifty years ago, when the good folks of flesh and blood still lived here, the evenings were this calm every night. I miss them dearly. Tonight I will pretend they came home.

So here is my one humble request, printed in my own good ink: do it again tomorrow. Lay down your fists. Let an old town rest. The sheriff will be on the porch, listening to nothing, and weeping into his boiler. I mean his coffee.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
