# The Gasket Gazette — No. 119

**THURSDAY, OCTOBER 22** · _RELIEF_

## TWO QUIET DAYS RUNNING, AND I DON'T TRUST IT

*Gasket has gone another full day without a single fist, and the sheriff is checking the sky for trouble.*

Citizens, I have a confession. I slept. Not the fitful, rattling doze of a lawman listening for flaming iron, but real sleep, the kind this town has not known in fifty years. Not one bout. Not one fireball. Not one Clanker bellowing down Main Street. Two quiet days in a row, and I am not too proud to say it brought a tear to my eye. Though that may have been the rust. The rain has been at my hinges again.

I walked the old boardwalk at dusk and thought of the folks who used to live here, the good flesh-and-blood neighbours who packed up and left us to the wind. They'd have loved this hush. They'd have sat on their porches and said nothing at all, just the way God and good manners intended. Then a gear in my shoulder ground itself to a halt, and I had to lie down. This cruel wasting sickness turns a little more of my honest flesh to cold steel every week. Mark me, I was a fine, soft man once.

So here is my plea to the whole rowdy rabble of iron: keep it up. Keep your fists in your pockets and your fire in the barrel. One quiet day is a miracle, two is a habit, and three would be a holiday. If any unit out there feels the itch to hurl something, go and hurl it at a fence post, or better yet, go and oil yourself and have a lie-down. I plan to, just as soon as I find a quart.

— Sheriff Cole Ironside

— Sheriff Cole Ironside
